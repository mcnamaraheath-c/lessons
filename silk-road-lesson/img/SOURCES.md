# Sources for the Silk Road lesson's historical images

All images below were fetched from Wikimedia Commons on **2026-08-30**. Each was
**verified visually** before committing (opened and checked against its description) and
its license was verified by fetching the Commons file-description page and reading its
license box. Note on method: the Commons **API** (action=query, extmetadata — the method
used for the Salem lesson) returned HTTP 429 (rate-limited) from this environment all
day, so license verification used the file pages' rendered license templates instead;
the template names found are recorded per image below. The underlying artworks are all
centuries old; the one modern *photograph of a 3-D object* (the sancai camel) is CC
licensed and attributed, since PD-Art does not apply to photos of 3-D works.

Processing: each committed copy was downscaled to at most 1400 px on the long edge and
re-saved as JPEG quality 72 (Python PIL, Lanczos). Original pixel dimensions are
recorded below.

---

## 1. `zhang_qian_mural.jpg` — Zhang Qian's expedition, Mogao Cave 323 mural (Tang, 8th c.)

- What it is: Detail of the Tang-dynasty mural in Mogao Cave 323 near Dunhuang
  depicting Emperor Wu of Han sending Zhang Qian on his expedition to the Western
  Regions (2nd c. BCE event, painted ~8th c. CE). Verified visually: ochre cave-mural
  detail with a mounted emperor, kneeling envoys, an inscription cartouche.
- Used in-lesson: Dunhuang stop.
- Source page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ZhangQianTravels.jpg
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/38/ZhangQianTravels.jpg
- License, from the file page (fetched 2026-08-30): Public domain — templates
  `PD-Art` / `PD-old` (mural is ~8th century; faithful reproduction of a 2-D work).
- Original pixel dimensions: 725 × 528 (committed unscaled, re-saved q72)

## 2. `diamond_sutra_868.jpg` — The Diamond Sutra frontispiece and text, 868 CE

- What it is: The frontispiece and opening text of the Diamond Sutra scroll, woodblock-
  printed on paper, dated 868 CE — the world's oldest dated printed book, found in the
  Library Cave (Cave 17) at Dunhuang. Verified visually: the famous frontispiece of the
  Buddha preaching, with columns of printed Chinese text.
- Used in-lesson: Dunhuang stop.
- Source page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Diamond_Sutra_of_868_AD_-_The_Diamond_Sutra_(868),_frontispiece_and_text_-_BL_Or._8210-P.2.jpg
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a2/Diamond_Sutra_of_868_AD_-_The_Diamond_Sutra_%28868%29%2C_frontispiece_and_text_-_BL_Or._8210-P.2.jpg
- Holding institution (per file name/page): British Library, Or. 8210/P.2.
- License, from the file page (fetched 2026-08-30): Public domain (9th-century work;
  faithful 2-D reproduction).
- Original pixel dimensions: 1610 × 969 (committed 1400 × 843)

## 3. `sancai_camel_musicians.jpg` — Tang sancai glazed camel with musicians, 723 CE

- What it is: Museum photograph of the Tang three-color (sancai) glazed pottery camel
  carrying a troupe of musicians, dated 723 CE, unearthed 1959 at Zhongbao village,
  Xi'an; the type piece is held by the Shaanxi History Museum. Verified visually:
  glazed Bactrian camel with bearded musician figures and striped saddle blanket,
  photographed in a display case.
- Used in-lesson: Chang'an stop.
- Source page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sancai-glazed_foreign_musicians_on_camel._723_CE.jpg
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Sancai-glazed_foreign_musicians_on_camel._723_CE.jpg
- ⚠️ License: **CC BY-SA 4.0**, photo by **John Hill** (from the file page, fetched
  2026-08-30). PD-Art does NOT apply (photo of a 3-D object), so this is the one image
  in the lesson requiring attribution: the in-lesson caption credits "Photo: John Hill,
  CC BY-SA 4.0" in both languages. Share-alike terms apply if the lesson is ever sold
  or redistributed (same caveat as the Salem lesson's CC photos — check before TPT).
- Original pixel dimensions: 737 × 1024 (committed unscaled, re-saved q72)

## 4. `byzantine_quadriga_silk.jpg` — The quadriga silk ("Shroud of Charlemagne"), Constantinople, c. 800

- What it is: Byzantine polychrome samite silk woven in Constantinople in the late
  8th/early 9th century, showing a victorious charioteer with a four-horse chariot
  (quadriga) in a roundel; later used in Charlemagne's reliquary at Aachen. Held at the
  Musée de Cluny (musée national du Moyen Âge), Paris. Verified visually: blue-and-tan
  silk weave, charioteer roundel, worn patches consistent with a 1200-year-old textile.
- Used in-lesson: Constantinople stop (woven in the very city and era of the lesson).
- Source page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shroud_of_Charlemagne_manufactured_in_Constantinople_814.jpg
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/Shroud_of_Charlemagne_manufactured_in_Constantinople_814.jpg
- License, from the file page (fetched 2026-08-30): Public domain — templates
  `PD-Art` / `PD-old-100` (9th-century textile, 2-D reproduction).
- Original pixel dimensions: 2019 × 2057 (committed 1374 × 1400)

## 5. `baghdad_caravan_1237.jpg` — Caravan scene by al-Wasiti, Baghdad, 1237

- What it is: The caravan (pilgrimage) scene painted by Yahya ibn Mahmud al-Wasiti in
  the 1237 Maqamat of al-Hariri manuscript, produced in Baghdad — camels, banners, and
  long trumpets. Held at the Bibliothèque nationale de France (ms. Arabe 5847).
  **Honest-labeling note:** the Commons file is named "…al-Wâsitî 005" and is sometimes
  described online as the "library" miniature from the same manuscript; on visual
  inspection it is the CARAVAN scene, and it is committed and captioned as such (the
  filename here says caravan). The in-lesson caption also states plainly that it was
  painted four centuries after the lesson's c.-800 setting.
- Used in-lesson: Baghdad stop.
- Source page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yahyâ_ibn_Mahmûd_al-Wâsitî_005.jpg
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d1/Yahy%C3%A2_ibn_Mahm%C3%BBd_al-W%C3%A2sit%C3%AE_005.jpg
- License, from the file page (fetched 2026-08-30): Public domain — `PD-Art`
  (13th-century manuscript painting).
- Original pixel dimensions: 2024 × 2009 (committed 1400 × 1390)

## 6. `afrasiab_mural_west_wall.jpg` — Afrasiab ambassadors' mural, west wall, Samarkand, 7th c.

- What it is: The west wall of the 7th-century Sogdian "Ambassadors' Painting" from the
  Afrasiab hall in old Samarkand — a long procession of ambassadors in patterned silk
  robes on a blue ground. Museum of Afrasiab, Samarkand. Verified visually: full
  panoramic wall photo, rows of robed figures, ornamental base band.
- Used in-lesson: Samarkand stop.
- Source page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Afrasiab_West_Wall.jpg
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/be/Afrasiab_West_Wall.jpg
- License, from the file page (fetched 2026-08-30): Public domain — templates
  `PD-Art` / `PD-old-100-expired` (7th-century mural, 2-D reproduction).
- Original pixel dimensions: 5750 × 1328 (committed 2000 × 462 — the 1400 px rule was
  relaxed to 2000 px for this one file because it is a panorama; at 1400 the figures
  stopped being readable in the lightbox).
- A second candidate was fetched and rejected: the single-figure fragment
  "Turkish dignitaries visiting king Varkhuman…" (Commons, PD, 766 × 912) — verified
  visually but too damaged/fragmentary to read for 7th graders; not committed.
