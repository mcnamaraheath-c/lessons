A Town on Edge

Salem Village was a tense place in 1692. Several years of extra cold winters and crop failures had plagued the town. A recent smallpox epidemic and violent conflict with nearby Indigenous groups created a culture of fear and suspicion. The small town was populated mostly by Puritans, who lived a strict, religious way of life — and small differences in behavior or appearance made some Puritans nervous.

What Do Modern Historians Think?

If witchcraft is ruled out, what was really afflicting the Salem girls? Historians have proposed several theories:

• Mass hysteria — a group of people all suffering the same symptoms without a physical cause, often under extreme stress.
• Attention and power — young girls with little power or freedom in Puritan society may have been drawn to the attention the accusations brought.
• Poisoned rye — a fungus growing on rye grain can cause hallucinations and twitching in people who eat it.
• Settling scores — accusations may have been a way for the town to get rid of people seen as threats to its values.

1. Which of these made Salem such a fearful, suspicious place in 1692?
2. Which of these is NOT a modern theory about what afflicted the girls?
