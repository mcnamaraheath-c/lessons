Meet the Three Sides

Patriot

"The colonies should run themselves."

Patriots wanted the colonies to make their own laws. They believed Britain treated the colonies unfairly.

They said, "No new taxes unless we get a say!" Later, many Patriots wanted full independence from Britain.

independencebeing free to rule yourself. Spanish: independencia

Loyalist

"We should stay British."

Loyalists wanted to stay part of Britain. They believed the king and Parliament kept the colonies safe.

They thought protests were dangerous and against the law. Many Loyalists had jobs that depended on Britain, so they stayed loyal.

loyalstaying true to someone, like a king. Spanish: leal

Neutralist

"I am not picking a side."

Neutralists did not choose a side. They believed both sides had some good points, or they just wanted to be left alone.

Many worried about their farms, shops, and families. Neutralists asked questions instead of joining a fight. They stayed neutral.

neutralnot on either side. Spanish: neutral (same word!)
Think like a colonist: A blacksmith in Massachusetts might be a Patriot. A merchant who sells British cloth might be a Loyalist. A farmer far from any city might be a Neutralist. Any job could be on any side.

Part III

The Stamp Act, 1765

Your task: Read the three short chunks. Then fill every yellow blank in the Quick Check at the bottom.

1. What did it tax?

In 1765, Parliament passed a new law called the Stamp Act. It put a tax on paper. Newspapers, legal papers, and even playing cards had to carry a special stamp. To get the stamp, you had to pay money to Britain.

taxmoney people must pay to the government. Spanish: impuesto
You: Do you use paper in your job? A printer, a lawyer, or a shopkeeper would pay this tax again and again.

2. Why did Parliament pass it?

Britain had just won a long war in America called the French and Indian War (1754–1763). Wars cost a lot of money, and Britain owed a big debt. Britain said, "Our soldiers protected the colonies. The colonists should help pay." So Parliament made the colonists pay the new tax.

Parliamentthe group in Britain that makes laws, like Congress in the U.S. today. Spanish: Parlamento
debtmoney you owe and must pay back. Spanish: deuda

3. Why were colonists angry?

Colonists did not get to vote for anyone in Parliament. Nobody there represented them. They said: "No taxation without representation!" That means: "Do not tax us if we have no say."

Colonists protested in the streets and stopped buying British goods. Many refused to buy the stamps. In March 1766, Parliament gave up and repealed the Stamp Act.

representto speak and vote for a group of people. Spanish: representar
repealto cancel a law. Spanish: revocar / anular
